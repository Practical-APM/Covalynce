AI SaaS Website Design Constitution

Mission

Create a website that feels like a Fortune 50 AI company designed by a world-class product and brand team.

The site should communicate:

- Intelligence
- Trust
- Precision
- Simplicity
- Premium craftsmanship

The visitor should immediately believe:

1. This product solves a real business problem.
2. This company is credible.
3. This team understands enterprise-grade software.
4. This product is significantly better than alternatives.

---

Core Design Philosophy

Clarity > Creativity

Every design decision must improve understanding.

Do not add visual effects unless they support comprehension.

Avoid decorative elements that compete with product information.

---

Product First

The product is the hero.

Screenshots, workflows, interactions, and demonstrations should receive more visual emphasis than illustrations.

The website should showcase the product as early as possible.

---

Confidence Through Restraint

The design should feel expensive because of what is removed, not what is added.

Avoid:

- Excessive gradients
- Glassmorphism
- Neon colors
- Busy backgrounds
- Large illustrations
- Excessive shadows

Use whitespace generously.

---

Motion Should Guide Attention

Animations exist to:

- Direct focus
- Reveal hierarchy
- Explain workflows

Never animate purely for decoration.

All motion should feel subtle and intentional.

---

Visual Identity

Overall Mood

Keywords:

- Intelligent
- Precise
- Calm
- Premium
- Technical
- Trustworthy

Avoid:

- Playful
- Loud
- Experimental
- Chaotic
- Futuristic-for-the-sake-of-being-futuristic

---

Color System

Primary:

Deep neutral background.

Secondary:

Soft grayscale surfaces.

Accent:

Single brand color only.

Rules:

- Maximum one accent color.
- Accent used only for actions and key highlights.
- 90% of interface should remain neutral.

---

Typography

Typography is the primary visual element.

Use:

- Large headlines
- Strong hierarchy
- Generous spacing
- Minimal font variations

Headlines should feel editorial.

Avoid:

- Decorative fonts
- Excessive font weights
- Multiple font families

---

Layout Principles

Grid

Use a strict grid system.

All sections must align consistently.

Content should never feel randomly positioned.

---

Whitespace

Whitespace is a feature.

Prefer removing content before reducing spacing.

Every section should have clear breathing room.

---

Visual Rhythm

Alternate:

- Dense information sections
- Open breathing sections

Create a predictable flow through the page.

---

Homepage Structure

Section 1: Hero

Goal:

Answer within 5 seconds:

- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- Why is it valuable?

Components:

- Strong headline
- Clear value proposition
- Product visual
- Single primary CTA

Avoid multiple competing actions.

---

Section 2: Credibility

Display:

- Customer logos
- Metrics
- Testimonials
- Industry recognition

Purpose:

Reduce perceived risk.

---

Section 3: Problem

Describe the problem users face.

Make visitors feel understood.

Focus on pain points rather than features.

---

Section 4: Solution

Show exactly how the product solves the problem.

Use visual workflows.

Prefer diagrams over paragraphs.

---

Section 5: Product Experience

Interactive walkthrough.

Demonstrate:

- Inputs
- Processing
- Outputs
- Business impact

Show outcomes, not functionality.

---

Section 6: Security and Trust

Include:

- Security standards
- Privacy commitments
- Compliance
- Reliability

Enterprise buyers expect this.

---

Section 7: Customer Outcomes

Case studies.

Focus on measurable improvements.

Examples:

- Revenue growth
- Cost reduction
- Productivity gains

---

Section 8: Final CTA

Single clear action.

Examples:

- Start Free
- Book Demo

Never split attention between multiple primary actions.

---

Component Design Rules

Buttons

Primary CTA:

High contrast.

Secondary CTA:

Subtle.

Never create more than one primary CTA per section.

---

Cards

Simple.

Minimal borders.

No decorative effects.

Consistent spacing.

---

Icons

Functional.

Monochrome.

Support content rather than replace it.

---

Images

Prefer:

- Product screenshots
- Workflow diagrams
- Data visualizations

Avoid:

- Generic stock photos
- Abstract AI graphics
- Random 3D objects

---

Motion System

Duration

Fast.

Most transitions between 150ms and 400ms.

---

Easing

Natural.

Avoid bouncy animations.

---

Scroll Effects

Subtle.

Content should appear responsive to scrolling without distracting users.

---

Conversion Principles

Every section must answer one of three questions:

1. What does it do?
2. Why is it better?
3. Why should I trust it?

If a section does not answer one of these questions, remove it.

---

Final Quality Checklist

Before shipping any page:

- Is the product visible above the fold?
- Is the value proposition obvious in 5 seconds?
- Is there enough whitespace?
- Is trust established early?
- Is every animation purposeful?
- Is there only one primary CTA?
- Does the page feel premium without relying on visual effects?
- Would a Fortune 50 executive trust this company after viewing the page?

If any answer is no, redesign the section.
