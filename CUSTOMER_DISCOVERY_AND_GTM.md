# Objective

Before building a product, validate whether AI FinOps is a sufficiently painful and urgent problem that organizations are willing to pay for.

This document defines:

* Customer Discovery Process
* User Research Framework
* ICP Definition
* Design Partner Strategy
* Open Source Distribution Strategy
* Pricing Hypothesis
* Founder-Led Sales Process
* First 100 Customers Roadmap

---

# Founding Principle

We are not trying to validate whether AI costs money.

That is already proven.

We are validating whether organizations:

* Feel pain from AI costs
* Cannot solve the problem themselves
* Have budget to solve it
* Will pay for a solution

---

# Customer Discovery Goals

We must answer the following questions:

1. Do organizations know their AI spend?
2. Do organizations care about their AI spend?
3. Who owns AI spend?
4. How are organizations currently managing AI spend?
5. How frequently do AI cost surprises occur?
6. What solutions are currently being used?
7. Would organizations pay for visibility and governance?

---

The product moves forward only if the following conditions are met.

# Interview Rules

Do NOT pitch.

Do NOT explain the product.

Do NOT ask:

"Would you buy this?"

Instead:

Ask about existing behavior.

Past behavior predicts future purchases.

Opinions do not.

---

# Stop Conditions

Pause development if:

* Less than 20% willingness to pay
* Less than 5 design partners
* Less than 30% pain score >7
* No clear budget owner emerges

Validation is more important than product velocity.

---

# Final Principle

Do not build features.

Understand problems.

The goal is not to build a dashboard.

The goal is to become the control plane for enterprise AI spending.

If customers consistently describe the problem as painful, urgent, and unsolved, the market exists.

If they do not, pivot before significant engineering investment.
