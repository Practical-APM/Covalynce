# Purpose

This document exists to answer one question:

Can OpenCost AI become a durable company worth hundreds of millions or billions of dollars?

Or are we building a feature that will eventually be absorbed by a larger platform?

This document intentionally challenges every major assumption behind the company.

---

# The Most Important Founder Question

Why does this company deserve to exist?

Not:

Why is AI growing?

Not:

Why is AI expensive?

Instead:

Why should OpenCost AI exist if OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, Google, AWS, Datadog, and Cloudflare already exist?

If we cannot answer this question clearly, we should stop.

---

# The Core Problem

Organizations increasingly spend money across:

* OpenAI
* Anthropic
* Gemini
* Azure OpenAI
* AWS Bedrock
* Cursor
* GitHub Copilot
* Internal Agents

No single provider sees the complete picture.

This creates:

* Cost fragmentation
* Ownership confusion
* Governance gaps
* Budget unpredictability

The opportunity exists because the ecosystem is fragmented.

# Risk #7

AI Costs Collapse

Probability:

Very High

---

Common Founder Mistake

Assumption:

AI costs will continue increasing forever.

Reality:

Token costs will likely decrease significantly.

History:

* Storage became cheaper.
* Compute became cheaper.
* Bandwidth became cheaper.

AI will likely follow similar patterns.

---

Danger

If OpenCost only tracks AI costs, the market may shrink.

---

Required Response

Build around:

Governance

not

Cost Tracking

---

Conclusion

Critical risk.

# Brutally Honest Founder Assessment

Today:

The idea is interesting.

The pain appears real.

The category is emerging.

The market timing is favorable.

However:

The MVP itself is not valuable enough.

Dashboards are not companies.

Governance is a company.

Control planes are companies.

Infrastructure is a company.

The objective must never be:

"Build AI Cost Analytics."

The objective must be:

"Become the financial and governance control layer through which enterprise AI usage flows."

That is the version of OpenCost AI that can become a category-defining business.
